Annotation note
Name: Divyanshu
Clip: F05_dense_4k

I did not draw the boxes one by one in CVAT. There are 1,199 frames and a few hundred vehicles and people in most of them, so doing that by hand in the time I had was not realistic. I used a YOLO11-L model that was already trained on VisDrone, which is an aerial dataset of cars, bikes, and people. The weight file is models/yolo11l-visdrone.pt. The script looks for it there. I did not resize, rename, or re-save the jpg frames. They stayed 3840 by 2160, and no frame was skipped. The detector itself runs at an inference size of 1920, and the boxes it writes are already in the original 3840 by 2160 pixel coordinates. The model has more classes than this task, so I mapped them down to the two we need. Cars, vans, trucks, buses, bicycles, motorcycles, tricycles, and auto-rickshaws all went in as vehicle. Pedestrians went in as person. I left out the crowd class, because a crowd is not one object.

After the boxes were found, I linked them across frames so the same real object keeps the same instance id. The drone is moving the whole time, usually only a few pixels between frames, and for a short stretch near frame 1020 it moves closer to 40 pixels. If I matched boxes in the raw image, the camera motion would look like the objects were jumping. So I estimated how the camera moved from one frame to the next and matched after taking that out. A car that is just driving normally then stays easy to follow. Parked cars and people standing still also keep one id the whole time they are in view.

The model sometimes misses an object for a few frames and then finds it again. If that gap was 8 frames or less, I filled the missing boxes with a straight line between the two real boxes. That is about an eighth of a second, and at 60 fps the object has barely moved, so the filled box still sits on it. If the gap was longer than that, I left those frames empty. A longer hole usually means the object was fully hidden, and the instructions say not to write a box when you cannot see it at all. I only put a light smooth on stretches where the box exists on every frame, so a gap cannot pull a box halfway between two places.

On ids I tried to be careful in the way the instructions ask. Ids start at 1 and go up in the order things first show up. Vehicles and people share the same numbers, so there is never a vehicle 3 and a person 3. When something goes out of the picture, that id is finished. The last boxes are marked truncated and only cover the part still visible. If that same person or car comes back in later, it gets a new id. Hiding behind a bus or a tree, while still inside the picture, does not finish the id. Those hidden frames have no box, and the old id continues if the object comes out again close to where it should be. I kept that window to about half a second. If it shows up much farther away, I was not willing to guess, so it gets a new id. A wrong join is worse than a split.

Riders were the case I spent the most time on. The bike or scooter is always a vehicle. I also box the rider as a person only when the body clearly sticks up and reads as its own shape. When the person and the bike blend into one blob at this height, I box only the vehicle. People sitting inside a car, van, truck, or bus are not boxed, because you cannot see them as a separate figure. Each person in a group gets their own box. I never put one box around a crowd. Auto-rickshaws are vehicles. A car on a sign or a billboard is not a car, and I did not see the model boxing those.

Small and far objects were kept when the model found them on several frames in a row. A box that appears for only one or two frames is usually noise, so those were dropped. Some dark poles, shadows, and things on rooftops still look a bit like a person or a bike. If that box did not stay steady, I dropped it. If it held for a long run and still looked like a person, I kept it, because missing a real pedestrian is also wrong. I am less sure about a handful of those.

Occlusion is 0 when the object is basically fully visible, 1 when something in front covers up to about half of it, and 2 when more than half is covered. I judged that from how much a nearer box overlaps it. In this angled view, the thing lower in the frame is the nearer one. If the model still drew a box, I treated the object as partly visible. I did not invent a bigger box for the hidden part on every overlap. The box stays on what was detected.

A few things are still rough. The cement mixers and the tanker have bigger, looser boxes than the cars, and in the packed queue two boxes sometimes touch. I left those as the model drew them. I checked this by opening frames at random, including frames right next to each other, and by following single cars and people from when they enter to when they leave. The same white car, for example, keeps one id until it goes out the edge.

Running the model on all 1,199 frames took about 13 minutes. Linking the tracks took under a minute. Looking through samples and deciding the cases above took longer than the compute. Overall this was a few hours of work, most of it on the rules and on checking, not on clicking boxes.

To rebuild the labels, put the frames in the path at the top of annotate.py and the weights at models/yolo11l-visdrone.pt, then run python3 annotate.py track. That step only needs detections.npz, which is produced by python3 annotate.py detect.
